# PortalAlley 1.4.0 — photographic portal

This release replaces the hand-built "cartoon" alley as the default destination with a real
photogrammetry capture, and adds a second, honest way to show genuine Tokyo video. The old
alley is still in the app as a fallback.

The short version:

| | What you see | What it really is | Where it holds up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spatial** (default) | a narrow real alley, walls, pipes, drains, sky | a textured 3D mesh reconstructed from 626 photos | parallax, leaning, crouching, looking up, reaching through |
| **Cinematic** | real rainy Tokyo street at night, moving | a 10-second flat video on a screen 2.5 m behind the opening | standing 1.2–4 m back, roughly in front |
| Illustrated (fallback) | the v1.3 alley | procedural geometry + CC0 photo materials | everywhere; it's the old scene |

**The destination is not yet a rainy Tokyo back alley.** No downloadable, clearly licensed 3D
capture of a Tokyo alley could be fetched without a Sketchfab account (section 6). The spatial
scene is therefore a London alley, used to prove the rendering path, as the brief allowed.

---

## 1. Architecture evaluation

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Photogrammetry mesh in RealityKit** | **chosen** | A textured mesh is a normal RealityKit entity. It inherits the existing portal masking (occluder + shader clip), world anchoring, LiDAR occlusion, placement modes, resize/rotate and tiering with no new render pass. It is depth-correct from every angle the capture covers, and it can be measured and tested offline. |
| Gaussian splatting (MetalSplatter) | future path, not shipped | MetalSplatter renders splats on iOS with its own Metal pipeline. To use it here it would have to run as a custom pass (ARView `renderCallbacks.postProcess`). Portal masking, the occluder, the room-side LiDAR depth test and the near-plane threshold would all have to be re-implemented in that pass. None of that can be verified without a Mac or a test device, and splats of a large alley typically need 1–3 M gaussians, which is several hundred MB of GPU memory and sorting work on every frame. No licensed Tokyo splat suitable for redistribution was found either. Splats also do **not** add walkable range or moving people: they show the same frozen moment, from the same captured viewpoints, as a mesh. |
| Real video + reconstructed geometry | partially: rain only | Proper video-on-geometry needs footage with per-pixel depth or a matching reconstruction of the same place. The licensed Tokyo clips have neither. Projecting them onto the London mesh would be dishonest and look wrong. What *is* layered over the capture is procedural rain with real depth (section 3). |
| Depth-aware photographic rendering (2.5D) | rejected for now | Monocular depth estimated from a video produces "cardboard" layers that tear at edges as soon as you move a few centimetres. That is exactly the "flat layer in an obvious 3D position" the brief rules out. |
| Portal-relative camera transforms | reused | The destination is a child of the existing world root. Its pose comes from `DestinationMode.worldTransform(dims:scene:)`, which is extended to per-destination floor and ceiling apertures. The ARKit camera drives everything; there is no separate virtual camera. |
| ARKit compatibility | unchanged | Same `ARWorldTrackingConfiguration`, plane detection, scene reconstruction/occlusion on LiDAR devices, iOS 17+ deployment target. |

## 2. Spatial Mode: how the capture is used

- **Source.** "Woolwich Alley" by artfletch, a photogrammetry capture (626 photos, Sony a7R III,
  RealityCapture, May 2022) of the alley between Woolwich High Street and Mortgramit Square,
  London. CC BY 4.0. Original glb: `Vendor/captures/woolwich_alley/`, with `provenance.json`
  holding the URLs and the SHA-256.
- **Conversion** (`Tools/assetgen/capture.py`, run by `build.py`):
  1. Measure the floor height from the ground triangles.
  2. Transform into portal space. The entrance of the alley sits at the opening, looking down the
     22 m passage, and the ground sits 2 cm below your real floor.
  3. Crop everything in front of the occluder plane.
  4. Recompute normals and build a watertight low LOD (268 k triangles vs 706 k).
  5. Add a captured-palette sky dome and depth-placed rain sheets.
- **Scale** is the capture's metric scale. A unit test checks it: the passage is 1.62 m wide and
  the ground median is within ±15 cm of the floor.
- **Shading** is unlit (`captureUnlit`). The photo texture already contains the real lighting, so
  relighting it would double the shadows. Exposure, saturation and a rain-darkening term are
  tunable in the material.
- **Loading** is lazy. Only the active destination's mesh is parsed (on a background task). When
  you switch, the previous destination's container is released and the texture cache is purged.
  Low tier loads the low LOD.
- **Placement.** Wall, floor and ceiling all work. The floor and ceiling openings use the
  capture's own apertures (looking down from 4.5 m up, and up from 2.4 m). The
  room/destination clip code is the same as in v1.3.

What you can do with it (verified in the preview, `docs/validation/v1.4/`):

- **Left/right parallax:** near walls and pipes slide against the far end (`after_left`, `after_right`, `parallax.jpg`).
- **Look up and down:** the upper walls, eaves and lamps (`after_up`); crouch to see the drain and ground (`after_crouch`).
- **Lean in and reach through:** the alley fills the view with correct perspective (`after_close`).
- **Masking:** nothing appears outside the opening at any of these angles.

## 3. Cinematic Mode and the hybrid decision

- **Footage.** "Heavy rain in early spring in Tokyo" by Fumikas Sagisavas, Wikimedia Commons,
  CC BY 4.0. It is genuine Tokyo footage, 1280×720, 10.4 s. It was transcoded to H.264 by
  `Tools/fetch_footage.py`, which verifies the SHA-1 against Commons; the content is unaltered.
- **Presentation.** A `VideoMaterial` screen sits 2.5 m behind a **wall** opening, with a dark matte
  around it. `CinematicScreen.fit` sizes the screen so that it fills the opening from anywhere in
  a viewing zone: 1.2–4 m back, within ±35°, eye height 0.9–2 m. A unit test enforces this.
- **Honesty rules built in:**
  - Cinematic is offered only on wall portals. Floor and ceiling portals switch to Spatial.
  - "Look inside" is hidden.
  - If you lean closer than the zone, the app says "This is flat footage - step back to fill
    the window, or switch to Spatial to lean in" instead of letting you discover the edge.
  - Synthetic rain and city sound is ducked; the clip's own sound plays.
- **What it is not.** It is a window onto a film, not a space. Moving changes which part of the
  frame you see (`cinematic_b`), as with any window, but objects in the footage do not shift
  against each other. The 10 s loop has a visible cut.
- **Hybrid.** Over the capture, only rain is added, as streak sheets at several depths along the
  alley so that they parallax correctly. No pedestrians, cars or signs from the footage are
  composited into the 3D scene. There is no depth data for them, so any placement would be a
  flat cut-out at an obviously wrong position. That is the situation the brief said to avoid.

## 5. Performance and memory (estimates, not device measurements)

Nothing has been measured on an iPhone yet. These are the budgets the build was sized against:

| | High tier | Low tier |
|---|---|---|
| Capture triangles | 706 k | 268 k |
| Capture mesh file | 28 MB | 10 MB |
| Capture texture | 1024² (~5.6 MB with mips) | same |
| Video (Cinematic) | 720p H.264 hardware decode | same |

- v1.3's illustrated alley is 379 k triangles with roughly 95 MB of textures on high. The capture
  has about twice the triangles but far less texture memory. Only one destination is resident at a
  time.
- Bundled assets are 77 MB (v1.3: 35 MB), mostly the two capture LODs.
- The diagnostics panel (three-finger tap) reports fps mean/p95, % frames over budget, memory,
  thermal state and triangle count, and logs "Destination … ready in N s" on every load.
- The fallback is the Low tier (low LOD, half-size textures). The app switches to it
  automatically when iOS reports "serious" or "critical" thermal state; low fps alone does not
  switch it. You can force it in diagnostics.

Different iPhones are not assumed to be equal:

- Devices without LiDAR lose only room-side occlusion.
- The mesh path needs nothing beyond iOS 17 RealityKit.
- Video uses AVPlayer, which every supported iPhone decodes in hardware.

## 6. Getting real Tokyo captures (no purchase needed)

The discovery workflow (`scene-candidates.yml`) searched Sketchfab, Objaverse and Commons.
Downloadable, CC BY Tokyo/Japan photogrammetry exists, but it is not on the no-login Objaverse
mirror:

| Capture | Author | Faces | URL |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant in the morning – Tokyo, Japan | Azad Balabanian | 887 k | https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/restaurant-in-the-morning-tokyo-japan-3a48ad53e6284d37b817aa9e4fcc3002 |
| Scene near Kanda station, Tokyo – 神田駅高架下シーン | nari4D | 300 k | https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/scene-near-kanda-station-tokyo-a9267f5beac342c3acd990718d097d68 |
| Tokyo Alley 3 | Geopogo | 62 k | https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/tokyo-alley-3-ed537aa6aed24d249af239db9e0bf073 |
| Facade of building in Asakusa | tomburtonwood | 736 k | https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/facade-of-building-in-asakusa-tokyo-japan-4841485cda9c4905be3ee69aa4264d78 |

How to get them in:

1. Create a free Sketchfab account and copy your API token.
2. Add it as the GitHub secret `SKETCHFAB_TOKEN`.
3. Run **Actions → "Fetch a Sketchfab capture for review"** with the model UID (the hex at the end
   of the URL). `Tools/fetch_capture.py` refuses anything that is not CC0/CC BY/CC BY-SA or not
   author-downloadable, records provenance, and pushes the file to an `assets/capture-<uid>`
   branch for review.
4. Then add an entry to `CAPTURES` in `Tools/assetgen/capture.py`: entrance, forward, apertures.

Whether each one is a real *alley* walk-in (rather than a facade or a single storefront) has to be
checked on the downloaded file; the thumbnails suggest Kanda and Tokyo Alley 3 are the closest.

Paid alternatives (not bought): Sketchfab Store and CGTrader sell Tokyo street scans, usually
$20–$150 under a standard licence, and many are artist-modelled rather than captured. Capturing
your own alley with an iPhone (Polycam or Scaniverse, free tiers, LiDAR) is the most authentic
option and gives a clean licence. The pipeline accepts any glb.

For **night, rain and people**, the honest route is a capture made at night in the rain. A daytime
London scan can be darkened, but it cannot be made into a rainy Tokyo night.

## 7. Real limitations

**Movement**

- You look through the opening and can lean or reach a phone through it. You cannot walk the
  22 m alley: there is no locomotion, and the view veils at the threshold as in v1.3.
- Coverage is limited to where the photographer stood. The right-hand wall is captured less
  densely, and roof edges and tops are melted (visible in `after_floor`). For that reason the
  floor and ceiling portals look noticeably worse than the wall portal.
- Nothing moves except the procedural rain. There are no people, no cars, no flicker.

**Image quality**

- The redistributable copy has one 1024² texture, so the image is soft up close. The original
  archive on Sketchfab is sharper (free account needed).
- The lighting is baked daylight from an overcast London day. The rain overlay does not make the
  surfaces wet.

**Cinematic**

- Cinematic is flat. It is only correct inside the viewing zone, it has a loop cut, and the sound
  is the clip's own.

**Not yet verified**

- None of this has run on a device. Preview renders come from a WebGL stand-in that uses the same
  meshes, textures and shading code. RealityKit's tone mapping and the `VideoMaterial` brightness
  may differ.
- Frame rate, memory, load time and thermal behaviour are unmeasured; run checks 41–52 in
  PHONE_CHECKLIST.md.
